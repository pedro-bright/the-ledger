## Summary

At 12:01 a.m. Pacific time on May 2, 2023, the Writers Guild of America went on strike against the Hollywood studios and streaming companies after six weeks of contract talks failed.
Among its demands was a rule that AI could not write or rewrite scripts, could not be used as source material, and could not be trained on writers' work; the studios rejected it and offered annual meetings to discuss technology.
The strike lasted 148 days and ended on September 27 with a contract that set some of the first negotiated limits on generative AI in a major US entertainment labor agreement.

## What Happened

The WGA's Minimum Basic Agreement with the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers (AMPTP), which bargains for studios and streamers including Netflix, Amazon, Apple, Disney, Warner Bros. Discovery, NBCUniversal, Paramount and Sony, expired at midnight on May 1, 2023.
That day the guild's negotiating committee unanimously rejected the AMPTP's final offer, and the boards of the WGA West and WGA East voted unanimously to strike.
Members had authorized a strike with 97.85% of the vote.
The guild's announcement listed "stonewalling on free work for screenwriters and on AI for all writers" alongside pay, residuals and staffing as reasons for the walkout.

The guild published a chart of its proposals and the studios' responses as of May 1.
Under the heading "Artificial Intelligence," the WGA had proposed to "regulate use of artificial intelligence on MBA-covered projects: AI can't write or rewrite literary material; can't be used as source material; and MBA-covered material can't be used to train AI."
The chart recorded that the AMPTP "rejected our proposal" and "countered by offering annual meetings to discuss advancements in technology."
The AMPTP told TIME that AI "raises hard, important creative and legal questions for everyone" and required "a lot more discussion."
Pay and residuals in the streaming era were the guild's top priorities, and AI was one issue among several.

After the two sides reached a tentative agreement, the guild's leadership voted on September 26 to end the strike as of 12:01 a.m. on September 27, after 148 days.
Under the new contract, which runs from September 25, 2023, to May 1, 2026, AI cannot write or rewrite literary material and AI-generated material is not source material, so it cannot be used to undermine a writer's credit or separated rights.
A writer may use AI with the company's consent, but a company cannot require a writer to use it, and a company must disclose when material it gives a writer was generated by AI.
The guild reserved the right to assert that using writers' material to train AI is prohibited by the contract or other law, leaving the training question unresolved.

## Why It Matters

This was among the first major US strikes in which limits on generative AI were a stated demand, five months after ChatGPT's release, and it ended with some of the first negotiated AI terms in a major Hollywood union contract.
The actors' union SAG-AFTRA, which struck the studios from July to November 2023, won its own consent and payment rules for digital replicas, and the video game strike of 2024 and 2025 extended that fight to voice and likeness.

The terms protected writers' credit and pay against AI-generated material but did not stop studios from training models on scripts they own.
That gap, together with untested enforcement, is why the 2023 agreement is better read as the start of a negotiation over AI in creative work than as its resolution.
