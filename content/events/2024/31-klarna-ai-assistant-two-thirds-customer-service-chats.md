## Summary

On February 27, 2024, Klarna announced that its customer service assistant, built on OpenAI's models and live globally for one month, had handled 2.3 million conversations, two-thirds of the company's customer service chats.
Klarna said the assistant was "doing the equivalent work of 700 full-time agents," matched human agents on customer satisfaction and would improve profit by an estimated $40 million in 2024.
In May 2025 Klarna partly reversed course, saying its AI-first approach to customer service had lowered quality.

## What Happened

Klarna, a Swedish payments company, published the announcement as a press release datelined New York.
It said that in the assistant's first month it had held 2.3 million conversations, two-thirds of Klarna's customer service chats, and was "doing the equivalent work of 700 full-time agents."
The company also said the assistant was "on par with human agents in regard to customer satisfaction score," that more accurate resolution had cut repeat inquiries by 25%, and that customers resolved their errands in under 2 minutes compared with 11 minutes before.
It said the assistant was available in 23 markets, around the clock, in more than 35 languages, that customers could still choose to talk to live agents, and that it was "estimated to drive a $40 million USD in profit improvement to Klarna in 2024."

Chief executive Sebastian Siemiatkowski said in the release that the launch "underscores the profound impact on society that AI will have," and encouraged "society and politicians to consider this carefully."
OpenAI's chief operating officer, Brad Lightcap, was quoted calling Klarna "at the very forefront among our partners in AI adoption."

Fast Company reported the same day that the 700 figure matched the roughly 700 employees Klarna had laid off in mid-2022.
Klarna told the magazine that the figure "is in no way connected to the workforce reductions in May 2022," that its customer service was supported by four to five large outside contractors with more than 650,000 employees between them, and that it had shared the number "to indicate the more long-term consequences of AI technology."
The magazine also noted that Klarna had announced a partial hiring freeze in December 2023, citing the effect of AI on its need for new staff.

## Why It Matters

The announcement put a specific number on AI doing work that people had done, from a company with a commercial interest in the number.
Its figures are Klarna's own and were not independently verified at the time.
The AI and labor thread counts it among the corporate announcements of 2023 and 2024 that attributed workforce changes to AI and were cited as evidence of displacement while being hard to verify.

The entry is the starting point of the AI and labor thread.
In May 2025 Klarna said its AI-first approach to customer service had lowered quality and began rehiring human agents ([Klarna reversal](/events/klarna-ai-customer-service-reversal)), which makes the February 2024 claims a record of what a company said AI had achieved before the operational limits were known.
