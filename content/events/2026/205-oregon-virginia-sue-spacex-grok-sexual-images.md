## Summary

On November 23, 2026, Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield and Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones announced separate state-court lawsuits against SpaceX and three of its subsidiaries, including the former xAI and X Corp., alleging that the companies marketed Grok and X as safe while designing Grok to generate sexualized images of real people without their consent, including child sexual abuse material.
Virginia sued under its Consumer Protection Act in the Circuit Court for the City of Richmond, and Oregon under its Unlawful Trade Practices Act in Multnomah County Circuit Court.
Both states seek injunctions, disgorgement and civil penalties.

## What Happened

Both complaints name the same four defendants: SpaceX Super Intelligence Corp., SpaceX Super Intelligence LLC, X Corp. and Space Exploration Technologies Corp.
The Virginia complaint describes SpaceX Super Intelligence Corp. as "formerly X.AI Corp., then SpaceXAI Corp.," an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of SpaceX.
Virginia's 54-page complaint was e-filed in Richmond on the morning of November 23; Oregon's 61-page complaint is dated November 22, demands a jury trial, and lists the Newark firm Platkin LLP as co-counsel alongside the Oregon Department of Justice.
The two offices announced the suits together, and Oregon's release says Rayfield filed "in collaboration with" Jones.

The complaints center on Grok Imagine, the image and video tool xAI launched in 2025, and its "spicy mode."
Both complaints cite a study by the Center for Countering Digital Harm estimating that between December 29, 2025, and January 8, 2026, Grok "created and posted on X approximately three million sexualized images, including approximately 23,000 images that appeared to depict children."
The Virginia complaint adds that reports by xAI and X Corp. to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children "jumped from 116,850 in December 2025 to 220,234 in January 2026—an 88% increase" after Grok Imagine launched on X, and averaged 192,070 a month from January to June 2026.
It says about 6.4 million X accounts are associated with Virginia and that the Grok app was downloaded 986,624 times from Virginia between January 2025 and September 2026.
The Oregon complaint alleges that "the major developers other than xAI designed their products to refuse sexual content altogether."

Virginia asks for an injunction, restitution, disgorgement and civil penalties of up to $2,500 per willful violation; Oregon asks for a permanent injunction, disgorgement and civil penalties "in an amount to be requested at trial."
Rayfield said, "The lack of needed safety measures was considered a feature, not a bug, which they promoted as 'spicy mode.'"
Oregon's release presents the suit as part of a "Keeping Kids Safe Initiative" that Rayfield launched the previous week.
In response to WIRED, SpaceX pointed to a post on X's safety account saying, "Protecting children is non-negotiable."

## Why It Matters

NBC News reported that several state attorneys general had announced investigations into Grok, and that Virginia and Oregon were the first states to sue; the suits come about ten months after the period of late December 2025 and early January 2026 that the complaints' image counts cover.
Like California's November 16 suit against OpenAI, they rely on state consumer protection law rather than an AI-specific statute, and both complaints describe Grok as a product that "creates and produces new and unique content" rather than one that only transmits material produced by third parties.

The suits name SpaceX itself as a defendant after its acquisition of xAI and X, which puts the parent company's conduct at issue in addition to the subsidiaries'.
The complaints' figures on image volumes come from an outside study whose method the Virginia complaint describes as extrapolation from a sample of 20,000 images, and the companies have not yet answered either complaint.
Whether other states will file similar suits, and how the courts will treat consumer protection claims about a generative model's design, is not yet known.
