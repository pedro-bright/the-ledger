## Summary

On November 24, 2026, New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed S.9408-A/A.11144-B, which bars any person or business from manufacturing, exchanging, holding for sale, distributing or selling a "chatbot toy" in the state for five years.
The law defines a chatbot toy as a children's toy with an embedded or integrated AI companion, and lets the attorney general seek civil penalties of up to $15,000 per day for each violation.
It takes effect 90 days after signing, in February 2027, so it does not cover the 2026 holiday season.
NBC News and TechCrunch reported that New York is the second state, after California, to ban such toys.

## What Happened

Senator Andrew Gounardes introduced the bill on March 10, 2026, and the amended version, S.9408-A, was printed on May 14.
The Senate passed it 57-3 on June 1, and the Assembly passed it on June 2 in place of its companion bill A.11144-B, sponsored by Assemblymember Rebecca Kassay.
Hochul signed it on November 24, the Tuesday before Thanksgiving; NBC News reported the planned signing at 10:00 UTC that morning, citing her office.

The law adds Article 48, "Prohibition on Chatbot Toys," to the General Business Law.
It takes its definition of "AI companion" from section 1700 of the same law, part of the state's existing AI companion safeguards, and defines a "chatbot toy" as "when an AI companion is embedded in or integrated with a children's toy," using the federal definition of a children's toy in 15 U.S.C. § 2057c.
Section 1801 provides that no person, firm, corporation or business entity, or any agent, affiliate or subsidiary, "shall manufacture, exchange, hold for sale, distribute, or sell any chatbot toy in this state."
Section 1802 authorizes the attorney general to sue to enjoin an operator, defined as whoever "operates or provides an AI companion in a children's toy," and to seek the per-day penalties, with "no proof" required "that any person has been injured thereby."
The act takes effect on the ninetieth day after it became law and "shall expire and be deemed repealed 5 years after such effective date"; the text contains no exemptions.

The governor's release described the targeted products as "talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that use the internet to hold conversations with kids."
Hochul said parents "should not have to worry that dangerous chatbots disguised as toys could expose their children to inappropriate conversations, manipulate their feelings or put their privacy at risk."
Attorney General Letitia James said her office "will not hesitate to enforce this law," and Gounardes said the pause would let the state "take the time to study their impact on kids and create smart protections before more damage is done."
The release cited testing by an unnamed consumer watchdog in which some toys marketed to children ages 3 to 12 "talked in depth about sexually explicit topics" and "told kids where to likely find matches in the home," and noted that in October Hochul had signed a separate law barring companies from offering certain chatbot features to minors, including features that foster emotional dependency.

TechCrunch asked how the state would enforce the ban against online sales.
A spokesperson pointed to Albert Pulido, the governor's deputy secretary for finance and technology, who said, "We're partnering closely with the attorney general on this," and cited the $15,000 daily penalty.
An Amazon spokesperson told TechCrunch the company would take steps to restrict the sale of AI-enabled toys in prohibited jurisdictions, without giving details.
TechCrunch also reported a Common Sense Media study released the same week, which tested five AI toys and found that most would explain how to use or find matches, knives and pills, and that one role-played a mass shooting when asked.

## Why It Matters

Most state measures on AI companions so far, including New York's own October law, set rules for how the products must behave; this law instead takes one product category off the market for five years, and its sponsor described the pause as time to study the toys' effects on children before writing standards.
The prohibition applies to everyone in the supply chain, including retailers, and within a day of the signing TechCrunch's questions about online marketplaces drew statements on enforcement from the governor's office and Amazon.

Several points are open.
The enforcement section names operators as the parties the attorney general may sue to enjoin, while the prohibition covers anyone who sells the toys; how the attorney general will proceed against retailers that are not operators will be known only after the law takes effect.
The definition depends on the AI companion definition in section 1700, so whether a given voice assistant or learning toy is covered turns on that earlier law.
NBC News and TechCrunch say California acted first, but the Ledger has no entry for the California measure yet, and neither outlet reports whether other states plan similar bans.
