## Summary

On November 3, 2026, Google said that Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search would be "entirely off for K-12 student accounts until school administrators explicitly turn them on."
Because Search was on the list, a school that kept Google's AI products switched off would also leave its students without Google Search.
After criticism from a teachers' union and children's advocacy groups the same day, Google said schools could turn on Search by itself, but Search would still carry AI Overviews and AI Mode.
Google set no rollout date and said it would give schools specifics "in the coming months."

## What Happened

The change was announced in a post on Google's Keyword blog by Chris Phillips, vice president and general manager of education, published at 14:00 UTC on November 3.
Most of the post covered Guided Learning, a mode that coaches students through a problem step by step instead of giving the answer.
Google made Guided Learning available in Google Classroom worldwide that day and said a Search version would start as a pilot in select U.S. schools later in November.
The post then turned to school controls: "After listening to schools, teachers, leading researchers, and policymakers, we are now updating our approach to controls for Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search."
Until then, according to NBC News, Gemini products had been switched on automatically for schools using Google Classroom, and earlier in 2026 Google had turned on Gemini in Classroom for students of any age unless a district disabled it.

Phillips told The New York Times that "Search and AI are deeply integrated," TechCrunch reported, and that students at a school that restricted AI altogether "will not have access to search, and they'll see a message from their school that indicates it's not available."
TechCrunch reported that the default-off change was planned for next year.

Criticism followed within hours.
Randi Weingarten, president of the American Federation of Teachers, told NBC News: "It is unfortunate that Google has prioritized its business interests over the education of our children."
Josh Golin, executive director of the advocacy group Fairplay, said Google was attempting "to strong-arm schools into using its AI products" and urged schools to look for "more privacy-protective alternatives for search."
NBC News reported that in early October, 13 parent advocacy organizations, including the Digital Childhood Institute and Schools Beyond Screens, had published an open letter asking school districts to turn off Gemini and YouTube.

Later on November 3, in a statement posted to social media, Google said the announcements "give schools more choice, not less" and that schools could enable Search on its own without enabling Gemini products, NBC News reported.
Search would still include AI features, which Google said had been updated so that "AI Mode and AI Overviews will offer step-by-step guided learning through problems, so that students don't get quick answers to schoolwork."
No option to offer students Google Search without its AI features was announced.

## Why It Matters

Two months earlier, New York City had imposed a one-year moratorium on student-facing generative AI through eighth grade, and Los Angeles had followed by barring student use of AI tools on district devices.
Google's change came after districts had started restricting student AI use on their own, and it sets the terms for schools that want to do the same with Google products: AI is off until a school turns it on, but the choice is between Search with AI features and no Google Search at all.
NBC News described Google as having a dominant role in education technology, reported that the vast majority of Chromebooks are bought by school districts, and cited a 2023 Schoolytics report that nearly two-thirds of assignments use a Google product.
The announcement puts on record that, for K-12 student accounts, Google does not offer its search engine apart from its generative AI features.

Several things were not settled when the announcement was made.
Google gave no date for the change and did not say how many districts had already disabled Gemini.
NBC News noted that the SCALE Initiative at Stanford's Graduate School of Education has found no high-quality causal studies of student AI use, so the learning effect of the Guided Learning features that Google is building into Search has not been established.
Whether regulators or school districts respond to the bundling of Search with AI features is an open question.
