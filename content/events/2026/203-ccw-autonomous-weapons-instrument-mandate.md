## Summary

On November 20, 2026, the Seventh Review Conference of the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW) in Geneva adopted a decision directing its Group of Governmental Experts on lethal autonomous weapons systems "to elaborate, by consensus, an instrument, without prejudging its nature" and to report to the 2028 Meeting of High Contracting Parties.
The group is also to recommend "whether the High Contracting Parties should adopt the instrument as a binding protocol."
The adopted text does not use the word "negotiate," which appeared in an alternative proposal posted at the conference, and the Associated Press reported that states did not commit to the binding rules sought by UN Secretary-General António Guterres, many countries and activists.

## What Happened

The conference met in Geneva from November 16 to 20 under the presidency of Swiss Ambassador Julien Thöni.
According to the final document, states could not agree on the conference agenda, the rules of procedure, or the preamble and declaratory part of the Final Declaration, and the conference "was not in a position to conduct a formal review of the scope and operation of the Convention."
It did adopt the decisions section of the Final Declaration, including Decision 1 on autonomous weapons, at its final plenary meeting, which the document dates November 20; the AP reported the agreement on Saturday, November 21.
The text is posted as CCW/CONF.VII/CRP.1/Rev.3, adopted "as orally amended" and due to be reissued as CCW/CONF.VII/11.

Decision 1 has three main parts.
It endorses the "set of elements of an instrument, without prejudging its nature, and other possible measures" that the expert group adopted in 2026 (CCW/GGE.1/2026/3).
It directs the group to elaborate an instrument "that specifies international humanitarian law, including principles and requirements, applicable to lethal autonomous weapon systems, and its implementation, and other possible measures," and to make a recommendation on "the legal nature and specific form of the instrument."
It sets the working arrangements: decisions and the final report by consensus, 10 meeting days a year in Geneva, a progress update to the 2027 meeting of states parties, and 2027 sessions on March 1-5 and August 30 to September 3.

A separate proposal posted on the conference website (archived on November 16), with no sponsor named in the document, would have mandated the group "to negotiate a new Protocol to the Convention," to submit "a report containing the draft Protocol" to the 2028 meeting, and to meet for 15 days a year.
The AP reported that seven countries (Austria, Belgium, Brazil, Ireland, Mexico, New Zealand and Panama) had proposed launching negotiations before the meeting and that the effort fell short.
"Technology is moving faster than diplomacy," Brazilian Ambassador Frederico Meyer told the AP.

The AP's account, published on November 21 and carried by The Washington Post, said states had agreed to "elaborate" a possible accord by 2028 without committing to binding rules.
Izumi Nakamitsu, the UN High Representative for Disarmament Affairs, called the result a "significant achievement" and described a "glass half full or half empty" scenario, according to the AP.
Campaign groups were critical: Stop Killer Robots called it a "watered-down mandate," and Max Tegmark, founder of the Future of Life Institute, said, "States have agreed it is sufficient to just talk about the issue, for the 14th year, while these weapons enable atrocities."
The AP reported that advocacy groups blamed obstruction "largely by Russia," and that activists accused Russia and the United States of blocking stronger language.
Guterres, who leaves office at the end of 2026, had set a 2026 deadline for launching negotiations on autonomous weapons.

## Why It Matters

The CCW's expert group on autonomous weapons was established by the Fifth Review Conference (CCW/CONF.V/10).
The November decision moves its work from a set of "elements," which the conference endorsed, to elaborating an "instrument," and it asks the group to say by 2028 whether that instrument should become a binding protocol.
The group and the 2028 meeting both decide by consensus, which, as the AP noted, means any single state party can block an outcome.

The decision leaves the central question open: whether the instrument will be a legally binding protocol or take another form.
Compared with the negotiating proposal, the adopted text drops any reference to negotiation and to a draft protocol due in 2028, and gives the group 10 meeting days a year instead of 15.
Whether states that favor a binding treaty will pursue one outside the CCW is not known.
