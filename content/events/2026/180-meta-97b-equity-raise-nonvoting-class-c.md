## Summary

On October 26, 2026, Meta Platforms announced a plan to raise about $95 billion in equity to fund AI infrastructure, and increased it to about $97.5 billion when it priced the first offerings the same day.
The plan consists of $25 billion of Class A common stock sold through forward sale agreements, $12.5 billion of mandatory convertible preferred stock, and a proposed at-the-market program of about $60 billion, for which Meta said it had no agreement in place.
The program depends on a new non-voting Class C stock that Meta created the week before, and only the preferred offering paid Meta at the October 29 closing, about $12.5 billion net.
Nvidia and AMD said they would put $3 billion and $1 billion into the offering, in releases that also described Meta's use of their hardware.

## What Happened

Meta filed an automatic shelf registration on Form S-3 on October 26 and, at 7:46 a.m. Eastern time, a free writing prospectus containing its press release.
The release described about $35 billion of underwritten public offerings: $22.5 billion of Class A stock and 250,000,000 depositary shares representing about $12.5 billion of preferred stock.
It also said Meta "plans to launch an approximately $60 billion at-the-market" program "upon the commencement of trading" of its Class C stock.
The same passage states: "The Company has not entered into any agreement with respect to any such program, and the timing, size and terms of any such program, if established, have not been determined."
Semafor reported the same morning that Meta was issuing new stock for the first time since 2013.

The pricing term sheet, filed at 6:02 a.m. Eastern on October 27, gives October 26 as the pricing date and raises the common stock offering to $25.0 billion: 40,000,000 shares at $625.00, against a last sale price of $630.56.
The 250,000,000 depositary shares priced at $50.00 each, with each one representing a 1/20th interest in a share of 6.50% Series A Mandatory Convertible Preferred Stock.
Each preferred share converts around August 15, 2029 into between 1.3620 and 1.6000 shares of Class A stock, depending on the stock price, with an upper threshold of $734.2144, about 17.5% above the offering price.
Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs were the joint book-running managers, and the underwriters received 30-day options on a further 6,000,000 common shares and 37,500,000 depositary shares.
The $97.5 billion total is the sum of those two offerings and the proposed $60 billion program; Bloomberg's headline gave it as "Almost $98 Billion."

The three parts differ in when, or whether, they deliver cash.
The common shares sold to investors were borrowed from third parties by the forward sellers, Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs entities, and Meta's October 29 Form 8-K says it "will not initially receive any proceeds" from that sale.
Meta can settle the forward agreements on dates of its choosing "on or prior to November 19, 2027," and would receive about $24.9 billion on full settlement at the initial forward price of $622.91875 per share.
The preferred offering closed on October 29, and its prospectus supplement estimates net proceeds of about $12.5 billion, for general corporate purposes.

The Class C stock came first.
According to the prospectus, Meta's board approved a new certificate of incorporation on October 19 on the unanimous recommendation of a special committee of independent directors, and holders of a majority of the voting power approved it by written consent on October 20.
Meta filed the certificate in Delaware on October 21, with an effective date of January 19, 2027.
The board also declared a dividend of one Class C share for each Class A and Class B share, with record and payment dates still to be set.
Class A stock carries one vote per share, Class B carries ten, and Class C has "no voting rights, except as provided in the Amended and Restated Certificate of Incorporation or as required by law."
Once Class C is issued and other conditions are met, the preferred stock converts into Class C, and in certain circumstances Meta can settle the forward agreements entirely in Class C.
On the October 21 earnings call, chief financial officer Susan Li described the class, in TechCrunch's report, as "a publicly traded non-voting stock usable for capital raising, employee compensation, acquisitions, and other general corporate purposes while preserving the voting power of existing Class A and B shareholders."

The final prospectus names Nvidia, AMD, and Broadcom as "Cornerstone Investors" that "indicated an interest in purchasing up to an aggregate of $5.0 billion" of Class A stock "at the same price and on the same terms and conditions as the other purchasers," in indications it describes as not binding.
Nvidia's release, dated October 26, says "NVIDIA will invest $3 billion in Meta's equity offering" and that Meta intends to expand deployment of Vera Rubin systems, Vera CPUs, Spectrum-X Ethernet, and BlueField-4.
AMD's release, issued at 9:20 a.m. Eastern on October 27, says "AMD will invest $1 billion in Meta's equity offering" and that AMD Helios rack systems are running at Meta.
Neither release gives a dollar value or unit count for the deployments, and no document read for this entry states an amount for Broadcom.

Meta's release put the raise in the context of 2026 capital expenditures of $135 billion to $145 billion, to be funded through "a balanced mix of operating cash flow, investment-grade debt, strategic partnerships, and equity capital."
It cited $97 billion of operating cash flow in the first three quarters of 2026, about $82 billion of cash and marketable securities, and credit ratings of Aa3/AA-.
Its third-quarter results, published October 21, reported capital expenditures of $34.91 billion for the quarter, free cash flow of negative $2.12 billion, and long-term debt of $80.9 billion, up from $58.7 billion at the end of 2025.

## Why It Matters

The plan followed Alphabet's $84.75 billion raise in June 2026, which used a similar mix of mandatory convertible preferred stock, common stock, and an at-the-market program.
Meta's release describes equity as "a complementary source of capital" that diversifies its funding, and cites its cash flow and credit ratings in support.
Semafor's Liz Hoffman read the same set of stock sales differently: "The biggest cash-cow businesses in the world can no longer support it, and borrowing can no longer fill the gap."
The filings give evidence for each reading: Meta held Aa3/AA- ratings and produced $97 billion of operating cash flow in nine months, while its free cash flow for the latest quarter was negative.

The $97.5 billion figure is a plan and should be read as one.
At the October 29 closing, about $12.5 billion had reached Meta, about $24.9 billion was contracted for later settlement at Meta's discretion, and about $60 billion was a stated intention with no agreement behind it.

The non-voting class means that most of the plan, once Class C trades, can be sold without reducing the voting power of Class B stock, and the prospectus states that while Class B holds a majority of the vote, Mark Zuckerberg "will effectively control all matters submitted to our shareholders for a vote."
Semafor described this as his "desire to raise it without diluting his control stake."
The class was approved by written consent, without a stockholder meeting.
The offerings launched on the same morning as Anthropic's public S-1, which gave an entity of its co-founders 50.1% of the vote on most stockholder matters and also named Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley as lead underwriters.

The supplier purchases add to a record of chipmakers financing the buyers of their chips, which includes Nvidia's compute financing agreements.
They also run in both directions in AMD's case: under the February 2026 chip agreement reported by TechCrunch, AMD issued Meta a performance-based warrant for up to 160 million AMD shares.
The documents do not tie the $3 billion or $1 billion purchases to any specific order.
What is not known from the record so far: whether the cornerstone purchases were filled in the stated amounts, whether the underwriters exercised their options, and whether Meta will establish the at-the-market program on the terms it described.
