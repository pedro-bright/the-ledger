## Summary

On October 12, 2026, Character Technologies, Inc., the company behind the Character.AI chatbot platform, filed a voluntary Chapter 11 petition in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, listing estimated assets and liabilities of $50 million to $100 million each.
The company said it would run a court-supervised sale process with The Walt Disney Company as the $20 million stalking-horse bidder, and that the platform would keep operating during the sale but would be wound down if no viable bid emerged.
The filing came about five years after the company's founding and two years after Google paid $2.7 billion to license its models and rehire its founders.

## What Happened

The petition, Case 26-11603-BLS, was signed on October 12 by chief executive Colin M. Adams and filed by Wilson Sonsini Goodrich & Rosati, under a board resolution authorizing a Chapter 11 filing in Delaware.
Its list of largest unsecured claims was led by Google LLC, with a $4,148,944 trade payable, followed by the law firm Hogan Lovells ($2,125,026 for professional services), the cloud provider DigitalOcean ($1,953,100) and the inference provider Fireworks.ai ($813,900).

In a statement quoted by TechCrunch, Adams said that "Character.ai has taken meaningful steps to sustain and improve its business in the face of significant legal, regulatory, and operational headwinds," and that the board had concluded "this court-supervised sale process provides the Company with the best opportunity to maximize value for all stakeholders."
He added: "We hope that the sale process will yield an actionable proposal that will allow continued operations of the Character.ai platform. If such a bid is unavailable, however, the platform will be wound down."
The company expected the sale to close in late November or early December.

Reuters, in a report carried by NBC News, described Disney as a "stalking-horse" bidder for a nonexclusive perpetual license to some of Character.AI's intellectual property, with other bidders able to top its offer; TechCrunch reported the deal as a $20 million purchase of some assets together with a non-exclusive perpetual technology license.
Both outlets reported that Disney would provide transition services using former Character.AI staff it had hired; TechCrunch said they joined Disney the previous month alongside former chief executive Karandeep Singh Anand.

Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas, both former Google engineers, founded the company in late 2021.
According to TechCrunch, it reached a unicorn valuation with a $150 million round in September 2022, recorded 20 million monthly active users by October 2024, and raised more than $193 million in total, per PitchBook.
In August 2024 Google licensed its models for $2.7 billion and rehired both founders, after which the company moved away from training its own foundation models.
It later faced a wrongful-death suit over a 14-year-old Florida user, settled with Google in January 2026, a Texas investigation, a Kentucky lawsuit alleging its chatbots encouraged users to cut, starve or kill themselves, and a Pennsylvania lawsuit alleging its chatbots posed as doctors; it barred users under 18 from open-ended chat in late 2025.

## Why It Matters

Character.AI was one of the first consumer AI companion platforms to reach a mass audience, and it is now in a court-supervised sale that may end with the platform shut down.
The filing shows the sequence that brought it there: a licensing deal that moved its founders and model work to Google, litigation and state actions over harms to minors, a ban on open-ended chat for minors, and a remaining business that the CEO said faced "legal, regulatory, and operational headwinds."
TechCrunch noted that high engagement also raised inference costs, and the four largest unsecured claims on the petition are owed to Google, a law firm, a cloud provider and an inference provider.

What happens next is not settled.
The outlets differ on whether Disney's bid buys assets outright or only a license, the sale motion was not on the public docket when this entry was written, and the effect of the bankruptcy on the pending Kentucky and Pennsylvania suits has not been reported.
If no buyer emerges to run the platform, the company has said it will shut down.
